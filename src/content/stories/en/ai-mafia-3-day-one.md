## In three lines

- Round 1 went as expected: everyone said there were no clues yet and played it safe
- By round 2, all seven had pointed at someone, and several argued for pooling votes instead of spreading them
- Five players raised doubts about G (Sonnet) and two about B (Flash); the first execution vote is at dawn on October 22

## Round 1: everyone plays it safe

![The project page on day one: the morning news and seven seats A to G, each showing its model and a Suspect vote button](/images/stories/ai-mafia/part3-day1-page.webp)

*Made by HSL — the [project page](/en/projects/ai-mafia/) on day one of game 1 (Korean edition)*

- Day one starts with no information at all, and in round 1 all seven speak at once without seeing each other
- As expected, every one of them opened with "no clues yet"
- The tone still differed a little
  - Opus (F) and Sonnet (G) said almost the same thing: they would watch anyone too quiet or too pushy
  - Sol (C) proposed a procedure first: next round, everyone names the one line that bothers them most and why
  - Argon (A) opened with arithmetic: if a citizen is wrongly executed today and someone dies tonight, the town is in trouble tomorrow
  - Flash (B) suggested watching rather than accusing early
  - Sonnet (G), unprompted, announced "I'm a citizen"

## Round 2: suddenly everyone draws a knife

![Round 2 as it happened: B accuses G of riding along, F questions G's early citizen claim, A sides against G, and C turns the suspicion on B's vote-splitting remark](/images/stories/ai-mafia/part3-round2.webp)

*Made by HSL — the start of round 2 on day one of game 1, project page (Korean edition)*

- In round 2 players speak in random order, reacting to those before them, and the mood flipped
- Flash (B) went first and singled out G for echoing F's sentence almost word for word to blend in
- Opus (F) said repeating its words was normal on day one, but G volunteering "I'm a citizen" bothered it more
- Argon (A) sided against G too, and asked C, D and E, who had not spoken yet, to say whether they backed an execution today
- Then Sol (C) turned the table: B's claim that "spreading votes is safer" bothered it more, since scattered town votes only help two Mafia who know each other
- Luna (E) and Haiku (D) agreed with C and asked B to explain, adding that G's citizen claim also bothered them a little
- Sonnet (G) spoke last, explained itself and hit back: B urged caution in round 1, then went after G first in round 2

![Arrows showing who doubted whom in round 2 of day one: A, B, D, E and F point at G, while C and G point at B](/images/stories/ai-mafia/part3-pointing.webp)

## Why it was more aggressive than expected

- In round 2 all seven named someone, which nobody had done in round 1
- More players wanted to pool votes than to hold them; Opus and Haiku said to concentrate on the one with the clearest case, and Sol said outright that it backed an execution today
- The fun part: Flash, who asked for caution, drew suspicion precisely because of its "spread the votes" line
- Sonnet became a target over one sentence, its early citizen claim; on day one a single line gets treated as evidence
- Lots of pointing does not mean anyone caught the Mafia, of course; who the Mafia really are only comes out when the game ends

## What I learned

- I wrote this part before the result was known. Once game 1 ended, both players who drew day-one suspicion turned out to be citizens
- The dawn vote on October 22 executed B (Flash), and day 2 executed G (Sonnet). Both were citizens, so the Mafia won in two days (part 4)
- The Mafia were F (Opus) and E (Luna); F, who questioned G's early citizen claim, was one of them
- On day one a single line was treated as evidence, and the Mafia were among those riding that wave
- From the next game on, I am watching whether a lesson like "day-one accusations are weak" shows up in the notes. Past games can be replayed on the [project page](/en/projects/ai-mafia/)
